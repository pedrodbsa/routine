# December 10K A-Race Re-Pivot — Design

> Status: approved in session 2026-07-15, pending propagation to the protocol stack.
> Supersedes the October 25 peak in the post-HM plan. Body-composition goals are unchanged; this document only changes the fall running periodization and the nutrition posture that supports it.

## Decision summary

The A-race moves from the October 25 race to **São Silvestre de Coimbra, a ~10K on the evening of Saturday, December 12, 2026** — the same race the athlete ran last year. The October course is elevation-heavy and a poor venue for a personal best; December is the athlete's chosen target for the best 10K time he can produce.

The primary goal is an **absolute clock PR: break 49:57** on the São Silvestre course. Because that course is hilly (see below), a clock PR there requires arriving in roughly **sub-48 flat-course fitness** — a genuinely aggressive target from the athlete's current ~50:00 shape, but a clean one given a fully fuelled 13-week build. The October 25 race is repurposed as a **hard tune-up / benchmark race**, raced on effort rather than the clock, about seven weeks out.

## Course reality (last year's data — Garmin activity 21249237220)

- **Date/time:** Saturday December 13, 2025, 18:34 start (evening race). This year: December 12.
- **Result:** 9.81 km in 52:12, average HR 173 (the athlete's lab LT2), RPE 80, Garmin training-effect label VO2max. It was raced at threshold-plus, not leisure.
- **Elevation:** 150 m of climb, and it is not evenly distributed. The decisive feature is a **52 m climb concentrated in km 8** (that split blew out to 6:37/km versus ~5:10/km elsewhere). Km 1–2 are net downhill; km 10 finishes downhill.
- **Estimated course tax versus a flat course:** roughly 60–100 seconds over 10 km.

Caveats: the Garmin distance read 9.81 km, so the course may run slightly short of a certified 10K — the clock-PR comparison to the certified-flat 49:57 is therefore not perfectly apples-to-apples, but the athlete has chosen the clock number as the goal. The km-8 grade (~5 %, from 52 m over ~1 km) is an estimate and should be confirmed against the official course map before treadmill-incline targets are set.

## The governing physiological finding

The athlete's **10K race pace (5:00/km) sits exactly on his lab LT2 (172 bpm)**. He races at the top of his sustainable metabolic ceiling with almost no gear above it. Four independent research passes converged on this and its two consequences:

1. **A repetition run at 5:00/km is race-pace work, not threshold work.** True threshold sessions must sit *below* 5:00/km or they are simply repeated time-trials. Prior "sub-T" prescriptions that used ~5:00/km were effectively race-pace work mislabelled.
2. **His lab threshold implies ~sub-48 fitness already** (VDOT ~44–45), while his 10K *time* is only VDOT ~42 — a ~2-minute gap. That gap is unlikely to be aerobic engine; the likely limiters are **top-end/VO2max ceiling and race execution/pacing** (pacing discipline is a documented weakness — historical ~36 s rep spread). This is favourable for the sub-48 goal: the lever is VO2 ceiling + goal-pace specificity + pacing, not more base volume.

Anchor to the **HR band**; let pace drift faster across the build (same HR at a faster pace is the PR arc). Standing rules: **walk recovery, and judge every rep by end-of-rep HR, not average** — the sole exceptions are the over-under floats and the 30/30s, where running the recovery is the stimulus.

## Session library (rotate within each slot: "vary the surface, hold the target")

**Q1 — Threshold slot (the repeatable weekly quality):**
- Cruise intervals — 5–6 × 1 km @ 5:08–5:15, walk 60–75 s (bread-and-butter)
- Continuous sub-T tempo — 20–35 min @ 5:23–5:29
- Norwegian sub-threshold singles — 8–12 × 3 min or 10 × 400 m @ 5:23 (volume engine)
- Threshold ladder/pyramid — 3-4-5-4-3 min (doubles as a pacing drill)
- **Over-under floats — 6–8 × (1 km @ 5:00 / 1 km @ 5:20), continuous** ⭐ most race-specific for a threshold-limited runner; drags the turnpoint toward 4:47. Run the recovery.
- Progression run (Kenyan thirds)

**Q2 — VO2 / 10K-specific slot (the hard signature):**
- Build B (VO2): 5 × 1000 / 4 × 1200 @ 4:35–4:40 (workhorse) · VO2 pyramid · Billat 30/30 (jog the floats)
- **Uphill VO2 hill reps — 5–6 × 60–90 s @ 3–5K effort** ⭐ equals flat VO2 for ceiling, beats it for economy, lower impact, trains the course
- Build C (specific): goal-pace reps 5–6 × 1 km @ 4:47 · **Canova km-alternations 8–10 × (1 km @ 4:47 / 1 km @ 5:05)** ⭐ makes goal pace feel controlled · cutdown / broken-10K

**Course-specific (the differentiator versus a generic flat plan):**
- **km-8 "attack-and-recover" reps** ⭐⭐ highest-value single session — surge the climb by effort, then immediately re-accelerate to 4:47 over the crest so the climb costs ~15 s, not 45. Weeks 5–2 of the build.
- Uphill sustained threshold (strength-endurance) · rolling-terrain long run (default) · downhill economy (1–2 sessions total, **early**, to trigger the repeated-bout protective effect; masters-conservative on the quads)

**Always-on:** strides 2–3×/wk (never dropped; also the cold-evening race primer) · short hill sprints 8–10 s · **Mona fartlek** as the low-pressure "soft-markers day" valve.

## Session pairing and interference rules

- **One genuinely hard session per week** (the phase signature); the second quality is deliberately moderate/repeatable (threshold family). Never two maximal sessions inside 72 h. Masters recovery requires 48–72 h between hard efforts.
- **The long run is the third stimulus but stays mostly aerobic**, and its intensity dials *inverse* to the week's two qualities — never three hard days. When the long run carries an embedded tempo/goal-pace segment, it counts as one of the two hard days and Q2 drops to easy/strides that week.
- **Heavy legs ≥48 h before any VO2/goal-pace run** to avoid interference blunting run quality; consolidate lower-body strength onto a quality-run day so the following day can be genuine recovery.
- **Hill sprints, jump squats/plyo, and downhill reps are the same neuromuscular/eccentric stress — never stack two on one day or on consecutive days.** Downhill work stays ≥48 h from leg day and ≥2–3 days before any key quality or the race.
- **Keep heavy strength through the build** (taper 3 → 2 → 1×/wk); it is a major running-economy lever and directly serves the hills. Drop to mobility-only in the final ~10 days.

## Nutrition / body-composition across the transition

- The cut runs unchanged to August 30. Then the athlete **stops cutting** — the sub-48 goal and a calorie deficit cannot coexist.
- **Reverse diet Sep 1 – ~14:** raise calories ~100–150 kcal/week from ~2,000 toward maintenance (~2,400–2,600), letting weight stabilise.
- **Expect the scale to rise ~1–2 kg on refuelling.** That is glycogen and water, not fat — it is fast running weight and it is desirable. The single behavioural trap in the plan is a panic-cut in October; it must be pre-empted.
- **Build (Sep 15 – Dec 12):** eat at maintenance to slight surplus, carbohydrate periodised to quality days, protein ~1.8–2.2 g/kg. No deficit. Race at ~72–73 kg fuelled and lean.
- **Taper:** hold calories even as volume drops (glycogen loading); carb-load the final 2–3 days.

## Variety and adherence (evidence-based, bounded)

Variety improving adherence is supported (Sylvester 2016 RCT; novelty as a self-determination-theory need), but it works through *perceived* variety and must not become "muscle confusion" that destroys progressive overload and specificity. The reconciliation the plan uses:

- **Fixed slots, rotating formats** — same physiological target, different packaging.
- **Constrained autonomy** — on a given day, offer 2–3 equivalent formats that hit the same target and let the athlete choose. The Garmin daily-suggested workout may be one option, with a coach veto when its target conflicts with the phase.
- **Deliberately narrow the menu in Build C and the taper** — specificity must win at the end.
