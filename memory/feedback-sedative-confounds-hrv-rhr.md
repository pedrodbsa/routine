On 2026-10-22 HRV fell 79 → 56 and resting HR rose to 56 after a clean night (7.48 h, score 82, deep 25%). I called it Load + softening recovery after four training days. The athlete then said he had taken diazepam and diclofenac before bed for a neck contracture. Diazepam is known to lower HRV and raise resting HR, so the dip was a confounded signal and the rule had fired on it.

**Why:** A sleep shape that is clean (on-time onset, no wakes, plenty of deep sleep) beside a sharp HRV/RHR drop is the signature of something other than training load. Medication, alcohol and illness all fit it. The recovery markers are ground truth only when nothing is acting on them.

**How to apply:** When HRV or RHR moves sharply on a good night, ask about medication, alcohol and symptoms before crediting load ([[feedback-soft-markers-check-anxiety]] is the same habit for rumination). Tell the athlete to keep diazepam off the night before a race gate (Friday Oct 24 night for the Oct 25 10K) if he can avoid it, and if he takes it, read the gate with that caveat. Diazepam can also raise appetite, so a hunger spike on a sedative night is not a tier problem ([[feedback-post-dinner-snack-hunger]]). A neck spasm drops overhead press, carries and face pulls for the day.
