When the plan prescribes rice, the athlete typically eats it mixed with beans (arroz com feijão) (noted 2026-06-24). Beans add protein, carbohydrate, and fiber on top of (or in place of part of) the rice portion.

**Why:** It changes the macro accounting for every rice meal — bean protein is a bonus on a cut, but the added carbs can push the day over tier if not absorbed elsewhere.

**The rice is batch-cooked with olive oil** (stated 2026-08-05): **~1 tbsp per 1 cup of dry rice, spread across the whole batch.** At ~185 g dry per cup yielding ~555 g cooked, that is **≈ 2.4 g fat / 21 kcal per 100 g cooked rice** — so a typical 100–150 g portion carries only **~2.5–3.5 g fat**. Add it for accuracy; it changes nothing structural.

⚠ **Coach error worth not repeating:** on first hearing "1 tbsp of oil in the rice" I applied the tablespoon at *plate* scale rather than *batch* scale, inflating it ~4–5× to +13.5 g fat / +120 kcal per meal. On that inflated figure I briefly concluded (a) that several logged fat-floor misses were phantom and (b) that true TDEE was above the ~2,310 estimate. **Both were withdrawn.** The real per-day effect is ~12–15 kcal — noise. The lesson: when a fact is about *cooking method*, divide by the batch before turning it into a per-serving number. Same failure shape as the Jul 21 weigh-in-timestamp artifact — a plausible mechanism scaled wrong, briefly overturning a correct conclusion.

**How to apply:** For any rice meal, assume beans **and a little cooking oil** unless told otherwise. Cooked beans ≈ P9 C22 F0.5 per 100 g, typical mix-in ~80 g; oil ≈ 2.4 g fat per 100 g cooked rice. Put both in the meal's macros at write time, and **do not also prescribe pan oil for the same meal without checking** — that double-counts. The rice oil is far too small to carry the 60 g fat floor, so [[feedback-fat-floor-lean-fish-dinners]] stands as written. If the additions push carbs or calories over tier, trim a later meal's carbs (hold protein/fat floors) per [[feedback-adjust-day-on-skipped-session]]'s carb-first principle.
