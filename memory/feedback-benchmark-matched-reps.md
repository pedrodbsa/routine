When a quality session is prescribed as an exact repeat of an earlier one so it functions as a benchmark, compare it on the **matched, warmed-up reps** — not the whole-session average.

Jun 30 vs Jul 28 2026 (both 5 × 1000 m at 10K pace) read as −5 s/km on the session mean, which looks like a regression. It was not. Jul 28's opening rep was *prescribed* conservative (5:07/km at HR 156 against Jun 30's 4:54 at HR 160) to stop the session running away, and that single rep manufactured the entire gap. On reps 3–5 the two sessions were **4:45/km at HR 166.3 vs 4:46/km at HR 166.3** — indistinguishable.

**Why:** pace-at-HR is the metric, and it is only meaningful once HR has settled. Averaging in a deliberately under-dosed opener compares a pacing instruction against a physiological state. Also pull weather for both — air temperature and dew point can move in opposite directions and cancel, which is worth stating rather than assuming.

**How to apply:** report the matched-rep comparison as the headline, the whole-session numbers as context, and say plainly when the answer is "flat." Flat pace-at-HR through a cut with a volume trough is an acceptable outcome under the Phase 4–5 scoreboard, which asks for stability; improvement is a Phase 6 deliverable. Do not dress it up as progress. Related: [[feedback-pace-hr-calibration]], [[feedback-quality-session-periodization]].
