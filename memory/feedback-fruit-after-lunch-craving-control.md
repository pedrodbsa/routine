The athlete eats something sweet after lunch because the sweetness blunts cravings and reduces evening grazing (stated 2026-06-24). **From 2026-08-06 the vehicle is 0-cal gelatin, not fruit** — athlete's call, after trialling the swap ad hoc on 2026-08-05. The mechanism is unchanged; only the food changed.

**Why:** It is an appetite-control / adherence lever, the same role the fixed midafternoon protein snack plays. Gelatin delivers the same sweetness at zero calories, which frees the fruit's carbohydrate to be spent somewhere it does more work.

**How to apply:** Spec **0-cal gelatin** in the post-lunch slot and **fold the carbs that would have gone to fruit into lunch itself** (athlete's explicit instruction, 2026-08-06). That is a strict improvement on a training day — lunch is the post-workout meal, so the carbs land inside the recovery window instead of 30 min past it.

**The fold is not one-for-one.** Fruit is near-pure carbohydrate (~95 kcal per 22 g C); rice carrying the same carbs also brings ~2 g protein and ~2 g batch cooking oil (~113 kcal). The swap cannot be both calorie-neutral and carb-neutral — pick one, say which, and don't silently absorb the difference.

**Scope this narrowly — it is the post-lunch slot only.** Strawberries/mango stirred into oats ([[feedback-oats-fruit-mix-in]]) and the post-dinner skyr + berries dessert ([[feedback-post-dinner-snack-hunger]]) are untouched and serve different purposes (palatability; evening hunger). Do not generalise this into "no fruit" — that is the same over-generalisation error made with the no-breakfast-before-a-run rule.

**Consequence for the Fruit Dessert Swap mechanic** (`nutrition.md` and `meal-rotation.md`): that mechanic exists to trade lunch/dinner starch *down* in exchange for fruit as dessert. For the post-lunch slot it now runs in reverse — the starch goes back **up**. It remains live for the post-dinner slot. Related: [[feedback-starch-at-both-main-meals]].
