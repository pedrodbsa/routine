**No single feed should dominate the day.** Established 2026-07-30 across three re-tunes in forty minutes: the athlete rejected a 730 kcal lunch ("the calorie amount is too big"), then rejected the 881 kcal dinner that had been built to absorb it ("900 kcal for dinner seems excessive"), then asked for the snack to come back *up* to ~270. Neither "smaller snack" nor "bigger meals" was the real preference — **flatness was.** The accepted day ran 479 / 537 / 273 / 721.

**Why:** Concentrating a day's calories into one sitting reads as excessive to this athlete regardless of whether the daily totals are correct, which puts adherence at risk on plan days where adherence is otherwise reliable. There is a physiological argument in the same direction: the 881 kcal dinner required a 57 g protein bolus, past the point where extra grams add to a single feed's MPS response. Redistributing protein earlier is better practice, not merely a smaller plate.

**How to apply:** Target meals in a ~450–750 kcal band and the afternoon anchor at ~250–300. When a logged meal comes in light, **spread the shortfall across the remaining feeds rather than dumping it all into dinner** — that was the error that produced the 881. Check the resulting distribution before presenting it: if one feed is more than ~1.6× another, rebalance first. Also cap any single protein bolus near ~45 g and push the surplus earlier.
