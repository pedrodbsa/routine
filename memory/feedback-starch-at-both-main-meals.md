**Both lunch and dinner must contain a starch.** Removing rice or potato entirely from a main meal to save calories gets rejected — "I want rice with my lunch, I need some starches" (2026-07-30, after a re-tune had moved lunch's rice to dinner).

**Why:** Starch is how this athlete registers a meal as a meal. A zero-starch plate reads as a deficit even when the calories and protein are intact, which puts adherence at risk on exactly the plan days where adherence is otherwise reliable. It also matters physiologically at the post-workout meal and the evening before a quality session.

**How to apply:** When a meal has to come down in calories, **shrink the starch portion (150 g → 100 g cooked) or trim the accompaniment (beans, oil), never delete the starch**. Note the trap this creates: an athlete asking for a smaller lunch *and* for starch in it is asking for two incompatible things — spec the starch, then state plainly that the day got larger rather than smaller, and where the calories landed.
