> **REVISED 2026-06-23 — read this first.** NEAT (daily steps / walks) is now **opportunistic extra, not a counted deficit lever**. The athlete is time-constrained and will NOT reliably add a walk; do not prescribe compensatory NEAT, and do not treat steps as an adherence target. The deficit rests on the **plan-day calorie tiers + social-day damage control**. The "prefers activity over food" preference below still holds in spirit, but the operative lever is no longer steps. When a session is skipped, **re-tier the day's food down** rather than adding activity — see [[feedback-adjust-day-on-skipped-session]].

The athlete stated (2026-06-18) that **lowering food intake is hard for him and he would rather add exercise** to create the cut deficit. He asked specifically whether to "up the cardio."

**Why:** It reframes how to fix a fat-loss stall for this athlete. He already adheres to the weighed plan on non-plan-day eating ([[feedback-tracking-adherence]]), so the lever isn't his meals — and a deeper plan-day cut works against both his preference and his adherence.

**How to apply:**
- When the deficit needs to grow, reach first for **low-cost NEAT — a daily steps target (~8–10k) and/or easy Zone-2 walking** (~250–350 kcal/day at near-zero recovery cost). It does **not** touch ACWR or interfere with the quality runs.
- Do **not** answer "up the cardio" with **more hard running.** He runs 35–45 km/wk already; ACWR has run in caution (1.46 on Jun 18). Added running volume in a deficit spikes injury/overreaching risk and eats recovery the quality sessions need. Walking/NEAT is the right kind of "more exercise."
- Pair NEAT with **social-day damage control** (fewer drinks, protein first, skip fried/refined carbs) — that's the single highest-leverage move, because the social days are what erase the plan-day deficit.
- Be honest about the ceiling: NEAT + social control can carry ~250–400 kcal/day. If the scale still won't move after a fair trial on weeks with controlled social days, a plan-day food trim eventually has to come back — there's no free lunch. But it's the last lever, not the first.
