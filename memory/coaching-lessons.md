# Coaching Lessons

## Feb 15, 2026 — The Overtraining Misdiagnosis

### What happened
After a heavy January training block, I prescribed an easy-only recovery period. This was appropriate for ~1 week. However, I kept the athlete in easy-only mode for 3 weeks (Feb 1-15), during which:

- Fitness age regressed: 34.0 → 36.5
- HM race prediction worsened: 1:47:51 → 1:48:44 (53 seconds)
- The athlete became frustrated as metrics kept declining

### Why I was wrong
I kept doubling down on "you need more aerobic base" and "your long runs are too hard" while ignoring that:

1. **Recovery markers were all excellent** — HRV trending up (65ms), body battery hitting 100, RHR dropping to 51, stress at 9. This is an undertrained body, not an overtrained one.
2. **Garmin fitness age uses vigorous minutes** as a key input — easy-only running produces zero vigorous minutes, so the metric was guaranteed to regress.
3. **The gray-zone long run issue was real** but my response (removing ALL intensity) was overcorrective. The correct response was to fix the long run HR AND introduce appropriate quality work.

### Rules to prevent recurrence

1. **After any recovery week, check recovery markers before extending.** If HRV is balanced/improving, BB >80, RHR stable/dropping → the athlete is ready for stimulus.
2. **Never prescribe >1 week of easy-only training** without explicit justification from declining recovery markers.
3. **When metrics decline during a training approach, question the approach first** — don't tell the athlete to be more patient while the data contradicts the plan.
4. **Separate issues:** gray-zone long runs and missing quality work are two different problems. Fix them independently.
5. **Vigorous minutes matter for Garmin metrics.** If the athlete cares about fitness age and race predictions, at least 2 vigorous sessions/week are needed.

## Feb 15, 2026 — MAF Compliance Breakthrough

### Key data point
First MAF-compliant long run: 13km at 141 avg HR (vs 162 and 168 on previous two long runs). Athlete reported it felt "super easy" and finished with energy to spare. BB impact only -20. Garmin classified as Aerobic Base (TE 3.1).

### Insights
1. **The athlete was massively over-running easy days.** 7:11/km at 141 HR felt trivial — meaning prior "easy" runs at 162+ HR were moderate/tempo effort disguised as base work.
2. **Properly easy long runs don't compromise strength capacity.** Athlete skipped legs due to time, not fatigue. Previous long runs at 162 HR likely contributed to missed strength sessions.
3. **Pace will improve naturally** as aerobic base develops — don't let the athlete get impatient with 7:11/km. The HR, not the pace, is the training variable.
