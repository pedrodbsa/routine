On 2026-07-01 I read a softer morning (74 FAIR sleep, RHR +4 over 3 days, BB woke moderate) as "the one-day cost of Tuesday's quality." The athlete pushed back and was right: he'd woken mid-night ruminating on 2 fresh whiteheads + imminent travel and couldn't fall back (51 min awake). The data fit his read better than mine.

**Why:** The distinguishing signature matters. **Training fatigue** = depressed HRV, elevated RHR, low deep sleep. **Anxiety / situational stress** = a wakeful *maintenance* disruption (long awake time, mid-night waking) on otherwise-balanced HRV and normal deep sleep. On 2026-07-01 HRV was still mid-band (77) and deep sleep normal (18.1%) — only the 51-min awake stretch stood out. That's anxiety, not tired legs. Misattributing it to training would (a) miss the real, more actionable lever and (b) make `/report` misread accumulating training fatigue that isn't there.

**How to apply:** When markers soften, before crediting training load, check the sleep *shape* — a long awake-time / mid-night waking with balanced HRV + normal deep sleep points to stress/rumination (the athlete's live constraint is sleep-onset/maintenance, not fragmentation). Ask what's on his mind. Skin anxiety is a known trigger and is amplified during the [[project-isotretinoin-course]] (early small breakouts ~1 wk in are expected, not a flare — reassure, hold the no-pick rule). The intervention is stress/sleep-side (stimulus control for the mid-night waking; don't lie there fighting it), **not** down-dosing training — an easy aerobic run is itself a stress regulator. Only trim upcoming quality if the waking *repeats* over 2–3 nights.
