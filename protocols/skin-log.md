## Background

Recurring acne for over a year. Key history, from the athlete:

- **Onset coincided with starting padel** — sessions sometimes 4 h+. Likely an
  initiating trigger via prolonged sweat occlusion, salt, friction, and heat on
  the face, plus general physical load. **Padel has since been discontinued** —
  so if breakouts persist, the initiating trigger is gone and the *perpetuating*
  driver is something else.
- **Escalating topical treatment made it worse.** Reaction to breakouts was to
  add more and stronger products, up to **tazarotene** (the most potent topical
  retinoid). This likely impaired the skin barrier and set up a self-sustaining
  irritant/inflammatory cycle rather than resolving the acne.
- **April 2026 was the best skin.** Two things coincided: (a) post-HM stress
  dropped sharply and sleep improved, and (b) the athlete **stopped all
  skincare**. Skin improved markedly. The "stopped everything → improved" result
  is the single most informative data point on record.
- **Current sleep baseline is better** than a year ago — toddler sleeps through;
  the athlete sleeps the night but wakes early (~05:30–06:30). So chronic
  sleep-debt cortisol is a *smaller* active driver now than at onset, yet the
  skin issue persists.

## Working Hypotheses (most to least likely)

1. **Skin-barrier damage from over-treatment** — the leading *perpetuating*
   driver. Supported by the April "stopped skincare → improved" experiment.
2. **Padel sweat-occlusion** — the likely *initiating* trigger. Now removed;
   serves as a natural control going forward.
3. **Stress / sleep (cortisol)** — a real co-factor (April was the lowest-stress
   window of the spring), but probably supporting cast, not the lead.
4. **Diet / whey-dairy** — low. Ruled down by years of prior whey use without
   this issue.

> These rankings are provisional. `/report` updates them as weekly data
> accumulates. The point of the log is to let the evidence move them.
