# TRAINING PROTOCOL - MASTER

> Weekly structure, strength templates, progression, and the readiness rules. Loads live in `strength-exercises.md`. Phases 1-5 templates: `protocols/archive/training-phases-1-5.md`.

## Weekly Structure

Scheduling is fully flexible: any run or strength session can land on any day. The reference week below is a layout, not a constraint. Scheduling is anchored to § Double-Day Guidelines, the readiness decision tree and recovery between hard sessions.

## Phases 6-10 — Reverse, Build, Cut 3, Maintenance, Lean-out (Sep 21 2026 – Jul 26 2027)

**Legs / Pull / Push, 3 sessions every week, in every phase** (athlete's commitment). `/report` flags missed sessions; there is no automatic downshift. The only race-driven strength exception is **no heavy Legs in the 3 days before a hard effort** (Oct 25, the spring 10K).

**Running:** 4 core runs plus a bonus 5th (`running.md` § Weekly Rules). Saturday is rest (`[[feedback-saturday-is-rest-day]]`).

| Day | Run | Strength | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mon | Easy — treadmill control run (fortnightly) or outdoor easy + strides | **Push** | One G1 trip, back-to-back |
| Tue | Quality | — | No same-day lifting |
| Wed | Bonus easy 5-7 km if time | **Pull** | Pre-lunch double when the run happens |
| Thu | — | **Legs (PM)** | Compressed 3-slot session, ~35 min |
| Fri | Easy + strides (or rest if the bonus ran) | — | |
| Sat | Rest | — | Standing |
| Sun | Long 12-16 km | — | Planned progression finish ≤ every other week |

Legs sits on Thursday because on Wednesday it would follow the Sunday long run and the Tuesday quality, which § Cumulative-Load Rules forbid.

**Drop order on a compromised week** (athlete's call — run over Legs): bonus run → strides → **Legs** → quality (to easy) → long run shortened to 10 km → Push and Pull merged into one upper session. Never below one upper session and one run.

## Strength Focus

- **Legs are not a hypertrophy target** (athlete: genetically well-developed legs, no added size wanted). Leg training exists for force production, running economy and structural maintenance (§ Leg Day).
- Upper-body work stays mostly 8-12 reps, RPE 7-8 on working sets. Leg working sets stay at RIR 2-3, never to failure.

### Build, Maintenance and Lean-out (Phases 7, 9, 10)

- This block has to deliver the Aug 1 2027 load markers (bench 60 × 8, OHP 35 × 8, lat pulldown 110 × 12 × 4, bench dip +25 × 10, 5 strict pull-ups; `strength-exercises.md`). "Athletic, not bulky": arms, delts, chest and back grow; legs do not.
- **Build volume = the Push and Pull templates below, +1 set on every accessory slot (Push 3-6, Pull 3-7).** That gives side delts ~8, biceps and triceps ~7-8, and chest and back ~12 hard sets a week.
- Extra arm volume goes into the existing accessory slots, never a fourth session.

### Cut 3 (Phase 8, Jan 5 – Apr 12 2027)

Same split at **retention volume**: the templates as written (no added accessory set), compounds at full sets. Loads held across the cut are the lean-mass instrument (no Bod Pod re-test). Upper loads falling on 2+ lifts across two sessions → +100 kcal carbs (`nutrition.md` § Phase 8).

## Leg Day — Running-Optimized

**The session is slots 1, 2 and 4 (~35 min).** Slots 3 and 5 are optional add-ons when time allows, never a reason to skip the session. No calf work (`[[feedback-no-calf-training]]`), no box jumps (`[[feedback-no-box-jumps]]`).

The dose is heavy, low-rep, low-volume and explosive. That biases adaptation toward neural strength rather than fibre growth, and it has the strongest evidence for running economy. Avoid the 8-15 rep near-failure zone and accessory quad volume.

**1. Primary lower-body lift**

- 3-4 x 4-6 reps **heavy**, RIR 2-3, rest 2-3 min
- Alternate squat-dominant and hinge-dominant emphasis by week
- Options: Back Squat, Front Squat, Romanian Deadlift, Trap-Bar Deadlift, Hip Thrust (Leg Press on PT days)

**2. Explosive / plyometric block**

- 3-4 x 5 crisp, low-fatigue contacts; full rest between sets
- Options: light Jump Squats (default), Broad Jumps, Power A-Skips 3x20 m
- Stop the set the moment contacts lose snap. This block also carries the Achilles/calf-stiffness role, since calves are not trained directly.

**3. Single-leg — prehab dose (optional)**

- 2 x 8 reps/leg, light: a control slot, not a loading slot
- Options: Bulgarian Split Squat, Walking Lunges, Step-Ups

**4. Hamstring curl**

- 2 x 8-10 reps, moderate load, RIR 3 (hamstring-strain insurance for quality running)

**5. Core (optional)** — isometric 3 x 30-45 s; dynamic 3 x 10-12. Core otherwise lives in Push and Pull.

## Upper Push

1. **Horizontal press** — 4 x 8-12 (barbell bench press, fixed)
2. **Vertical press** — 3 x 8-12 (overhead press, fixed)
3. **Chest accessory** — 3 x 10-15
4. **Dips** — 3 x 8-12 (weighted bench dip, fixed)
5. **Lateral raises** — 3 x 12-15
6. **Triceps isolation** — 3 x 10-15 (cable pushdown or overhead cable extension)
7. **Core** — 7a anti-extension 3 sets; 7b anti-rotation or flexion 3 sets

## Upper Pull

1. **Vertical pull** — 4 x 8-12 lat pulldown (fixed). 110 kg is the stack's top, so progress by reps to 12 across, then by a 3-s lowering. **When a bar is available, open with pull-up practice:** 3-5 strict singles (dead hang to chin over the bar, 60-90 s rest) and 3 slow negatives (5 s down), then take the pulldown for 3 sets.
2. **Horizontal row** — 4 x 8-12
3. **Row accessory** — 3 x 10-12
4. **Face pulls** — 3 x 15-20
5. **Lateral raise** — 3 x 12-15 (the second side-delt exposure; rotate its implement against Push's)
6. **Curl variation** — 3 x 10-12
7. **Second biceps slot** — 2-3 x 10-12 (a different implement or angle from slot 6)
8. **Core** — 8a anti-rotation or flexion 3 sets; 8b carry or stability 3 sets

## Session Compression Priority

- **When the prior week ran <3/3, the current week opens with the most-overdue session type**, not the most convenient slot (convenience produced three Pushes in July while Pull went 3 weeks).
- **Never run the same session type twice while another type is >10 days old.** The overdue one takes the slot.

## Exercise Rotation Rules

- **The four scorecard lifts are fixed in their slots in every phase:** bench press (Push 1), overhead press (Push 2), weighted bench dip (Push 4), lat pulldown and pull-up practice (Pull 1). Rotation applies to the other slots.
- Never repeat the exact same selection of the other slots two weeks in a row; swap at least 2-3 of them when repeating the session type.
- Keep the movement pattern constant; change the implement or variation.
- Lateral raises, face pulls, jumps/plyo and some core slots can repeat.
- **Rotate against the last _two_ same-type sessions** in the daily files, not just the most recent — a one-session memory degenerates into an A/B flip-flop.

## Progression Rules

- Double progression (upper body): hit the top of the rep range with clean form → add load → reset to the lower end.
- **Legs: load progression at fixed reps, governed by RIR.** When the primary lift feels like RIR 4+ at the prescribed reps, add load; reps stay at 4-6. Never chase rep PRs or grind to failure.
- A lift regressing >10% for 2 sessions → cut one set before cutting the weight.
- RPE staying 9+ despite normal recovery → reduce load 5% and reassess calories and sleep.
- No logged loads means no progression decision.

## Deload Rules

- The 12-week build has no scheduled deload: the Nov 2–8 trekking week (build week 4) is its deload.
- Otherwise, an autoregulated mini-deload only when ACWR, soreness or sleep markers justify it. Keep intensity touches; remove junk volume.

## Double-Day Guidelines

The default training window is **before lunch** (athlete preference): run before breakfast, then the upper lift before lunch, or both combined. **Lunch is the post-workout meal** on these days. This fixes time of day, not weekday.

- Run first, lift second. Breakfast between the run and the lift is the lift's fuel. If both are done fasted back-to-back, take the post-run shake before the lift.
- The pre-lunch double suits an easy run + an upper session. Quality days carry no lifting.
- **Legs is the one PM session, typically Thursday.** Lunch fuels it; dinner is the post-workout meal. It needs ≥36 h before the next quality run.
- Low energy for the second session → drop 1 set per exercise before considering cancellation.
- Never schedule hard running and Legs on the same day.

## Rest-Day NEAT — Incline Walk (optional)

On a true rest day an optional incline-treadmill walk is available first thing. NEAT is opportunistic: never counted as a deficit lever, never prescribed to make up for a skipped session, and dropped without comment when time or recovery is short (Body Battery <30 at wake, or a <5 h night).

- Incline 8% (6% if HR runs high; progress toward 10%), 5.0 km/h, 40 min (progress time before incline, never speed)
- HR ≤125 — if it drifts over, lower incline or speed; do not jog
- Hands off the rails; fasted is fine, ~300 mL water first
- Yields ~300-350 kcal and ~4,000 steps

## Sleep and Readiness Decision Tree

Apply this before keeping, downgrading or cancelling training.

### Same-Day Rules

The normal night is **6-8 h**, capped by early waking (~5:30-6:30). Sleep-onset latency is the live constraint, and short nights follow late onsets. The normal band is the "train as planned" case; the rules below are for genuinely short nights.

- **Sleep <5 h:** full rest day, maintenance calories, mobility only.
- **Sleep 5-6 h:** no Legs. Quality converts to easy below 5.5 h; at 5.5-6 h it stands only if HRV, RHR and Body Battery are normal. Upper lift allowed at reduced volume if motivation is good.
- **Sleep 6-8 h:** train as planned unless other markers are poor or onset was badly delayed.

### Override Rules

- **Body Battery <30:** full rest day regardless of sleep.
- **Sleep score <60 for 2 nights:** skip the next quality session.
- **HRV >15% below the 7-day average for 3 days:** 2 easy or rest days.
- **RHR +5 bpm for 3 days:** reduce weekly volume 20%.
- **Heat + poor sleep:** easy running only.

### Cumulative-Load Rules (trailing multi-day)

The rules above read only this morning's markers. `/plan` also builds a 7-day ledger (sessions per day plus the HRV / RHR / Body Battery trend). This athlete trains six days a week by design, so a consecutive-day count is not a fatigue flag on its own; the flag is load *without* recovery:

- **Stacked hard sessions:** ≥2 hard sessions (quality, long run or heavy Legs) in the trailing 3 days → the next day is easy or rest, never hard.
- **Load + softening recovery:** consecutive training days with HRV drifting down, RHR up or Body Battery not recharging → default the next day to easy or rest, preferring rest when the session's value is low.
- **Race or hard effort within ~7 days:** bias toward freshness. Rest/easy is the default day, any quality is short and justified, and low-value easy volume is cut first.

These are defaults, not hard stops. Overriding one requires an explicit reason in the daily file's `## Context`.

## Minimum-Effective-Dose Fallback Week

For a whole week made unrealistic by family disruption, illness exposure or repeated sleep loss:

- 3 runs: 2 easy + 1 short quality touch or steady aerobic run
- 2 strength sessions: 20-30 min upper/full-body minimum dose
- Calories at the planned day type unless sleep is severely compromised (then maintenance for 1-2 days)
- Exit only after 2 nights of ≥6 h sleep and normal motivation

## Disrupted-Day Protocol

For single days (~4-6 a month) where social events, family demands or a short or late night make the full plan impractical:

- **Running:** keep the run if possible, shortened 20-30%. Quality → easy if motivation <3 or sleep <5.5 h.
- **Strength:** the 2-3 most important compounds, 2 sets each; skip accessories and core.
- **Nutrition:** the protein floor still applies; a social meal makes it the social/flex day.
- **Mobility:** hip flexor stretch, 90/90 and cat-cow instead of the full routine.
- A disrupted day at 60% beats a skipped day. Do not compensate with extra volume the next day.

## Tracking Rules

- Log every working weight.
- PT sessions count only if loads or a usable effort note are logged.
- Runs: distance, pace, avg HR and RPE. RPE is Garmin's post-activity perceived effort (`workout_rpe`, 10-100 = RPE 1-10), rated on the watch after every session, lifts included, and read by `/recap`.
