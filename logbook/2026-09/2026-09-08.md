**Why the two disagree.** Garmin's underlying VO2max estimate reads **48 on Sep 2, and it has read 48 since Jul 15** — flat across the entire slide. The endpoint returns integers only and updates sparsely, so sub-point drift is invisible and roughly 0.5–0.8 of a hidden point could account for the 54 s on its own; that caveat is real and is not being waved away. But the rest of the mechanism is not in doubt:

1. **The predictor's training-history window still contains the wreckage.** Aug 17–23 was 16.02 km, Aug 24–30 was 8.53 km, with gastroenteritis through the middle. Those two weeks are inside the ~4-week consistency window the race predictor weights, and they do not age out until roughly mid-September. The prediction is describing August, not today.
2. **Nothing has been fed to it that could push it up.** VO2max only meaningfully refreshes on efforts with real intensity. The last quality was Sep 2; since then there has been one long run and two easy runs, and this week is running-only with zero quality by rule. Easy work at 140 bpm cannot raise the estimate — it can only let it decay.
3. **RHR is +5.** An elevated autonomic state inflates heart rate at any given pace, and the algorithm reads that as worse economy. Today's 140 bpm at 6:40/km against Aug 30's 138 at 6:34/km is exactly the kind of 2 bpm penalty that feeds the estimate downward while meaning nothing about fitness.

**So: expect it to get worse before it gets better, and stop reading it daily.** This week is easy-only by design, the junk weeks have not aged out, and RHR is still up. A prediction that improves this week would be the surprising outcome. Checking it daily converts a lagging four-week aggregate into a mood input, which is the last thing a motivation-2 week needs.
